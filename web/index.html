    How this outlook is calculated

    The observed starting price is AAA's published regular gasoline metro average, with its actual source date. If your city has no matching metro, the app explicitly offers the state average.

    1. History: up to 160 EIA weekly observations, using a city series where available or a labeled state/region series.
    2. Markets: prior-week changes in WTI crude futures, RBOB gasoline futures, and the XLE energy stock ETF. Daily values are aligned to each historical forecast date; later observations are excluded.
    3. Model: a standardized ridge regression predicts the next weekly gas-price change. Its estimate is blended 70/30 with observed local momentum, damped across 14 days, and bounded by historical volatility.
    4. Three scenarios: the positive and negative paths use the lower and upper historical forecast-error quantiles, widening over time. The middle path is the central estimate. These are approximate scenarios, not calibrated probability guarantees.
    5. News: upward/downward headline keywords add a small bounded stress adjustment. Counts cover only retrieved articles. This heuristic adjustment has not been backtested.

    Validation: the most recent 16 eligible historical weeks are predicted one at a time using only earlier training data. The dashboard reports their mean absolute error and compares it with holding the previous week's price constant. This evaluates the weekly regression, not the daily interpolation or news adjustment. Missing market feeds are excluded; missing or stale price data suppress projections. No confidence percentage is invented.

    Map markers outside the selected metro use national history for a comparable first estimate. Selecting a metro loads its specific history and recalculates its projections. Futures rollovers, local tax differences, supply shocks, and mismatched survey areas can all affect accuracy.
